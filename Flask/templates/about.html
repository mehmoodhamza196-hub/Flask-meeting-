Meter About

Every meeting has a price. Now you can see it.

Meeting Cost Meter turns the time your team spends together into a live running total, so you can decide whether a meeting is worth its cost while it is still happening.

How the cost is calculated

The meter multiplies the number of attendees by the average rate per person, then by the time that has passed.

Cost = attendees × rate per minute × minutes elapsed

Example: 6 people at 40 per hour (0.67 per minute) for 30 minutes costs 6 × 0.67 × 30 = 120.

What you can do with it

  • Start, pause, resume and reset the clock as the meeting runs.
  • Enter the average rate per hour or per minute. Changing one updates the other.
  • Change the number of attendees or the rate mid-meeting and see the total adjust at once.
  • Switch between US dollar, Pakistani rupee, euro, British pound, Indian rupee and UAE dirham.
  • Copy a one-line summary to paste into a report, a chat or the meeting notes.

Who it is for

Team leads who want shorter standups, organizers deciding whether a call should be an email, facilitators who need a fair reason to wrap up, and freelancers who want to show clients the cost of extra calls.

Good to know

  • The meter uses one average rate for everyone, so it gives an estimate, not an exact payroll figure.
  • It measures time cost only. It does not include the cost of interrupted work or preparation.
  • Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you enter is saved or sent to a server.

Questions

What rate should I enter?

Use the average hourly cost of the people in the room. If you do not know salaries, a rough figure for the typical role works. Add overhead if you want the full cost of employment.

Does the timer keep running if I close the tab?

No. The timer lives in the open page, so closing or refreshing it resets the meeting. Copy the summary before you leave.

Can I use it for one-to-one calls?

Yes. Set attendees to 2 and enter the average rate of both people.
